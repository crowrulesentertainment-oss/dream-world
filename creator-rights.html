THE DREAM WORLD PRINCIPLE

We don't just collect dreams. We build them with the people who dreamed them.

A submitted dream may begin as a simple idea, strange image, nightmare, character, scene or story. If CrowRules chooses to develop it, the goal is to work with the creator to shape that idea into something real. The project can evolve over time, and the creator's involvement, credit, compensation and rights should be documented through the appropriate project agreements.

Core Rights Principles

These principles describe how Dream World intends to organize creative relationships. The applicable written agreement for a particular project controls the actual contractual terms.

Your Submission

Submitting a dream does not, by itself, automatically mean that CrowRules owns everything associated with the submission. The applicable submission terms and later project agreements determine the relationship.

Submission Record

Your Creative Expression

Written stories, scripts, artwork, recordings and other creative expression may involve rights distinct from the underlying idea. Those contributions should be identified and documented appropriately.

Development

Credits

Credit Record

Compensation

Compensation & Participation

Compensation is project-specific and should be based on actual written agreements rather than assumptions or promises.

Development

Agreement

Development payments, if any, are governed by the applicable project agreement.

Creative Work

Defined

NO AUTOMATIC PROMISE OF PAYMENT

A dream submission does not automatically create a promise of payment, ownership, royalties or production. If CrowRules and a creator agree to compensation or participation, the terms should be clearly documented in a project-specific written agreement.

IMPORTANT RIGHTS NOTICE

This page is an organizational and educational overview, not a substitute for a negotiated legal agreement or legal advice.
